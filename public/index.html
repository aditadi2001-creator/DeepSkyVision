Bangalore’s sky is bright. The signal is still there.

I shoot from a Bortle 9 rooftop, about as light-polluted as skies get. Narrowband filters pass only the light that hydrogen, oxygen and sulphur atoms emit, so nebulae still stand out against the city glow.

It takes more hours than a dark site would. This site is where those hours end up: finished frames, the gear behind them, and the files for the projects I build along the way.

    From photons to picture

    1. Capture

      N.I.N.A. runs the night: framing, focusing and dithering, with PHD2 guiding. The C9.25 collects hundreds of short frames through narrowband and broadband filters.

    2. Calibrate

      Dark and flat frames remove sensor noise and the shadows of dust. The FlatCalibration Panel is being built to automate this.

    3. Integrate

      PixInsight aligns and stacks every frame. Signal adds up steadily while random noise doesn’t, which is why hours beat minutes.

    4. Process

      Gradient removal, sharpening, stretching and colour mapping, then final touches in Photoshop. Narrowband data is mapped to the SHO palette.

    Full gear list
    Telescope
    Celestron C9.25 with focal reducer, about 1210 mm at f/5.15
    Mount
    ZWO AM5N on a rooftop pier
    Camera
    ToupTek ATR585M mono (Sony IMX585)
    Filters
    ZWO Hα, OIII, SII narrowband set, Optolong L-Pro, LRGB
    Software
    N.I.N.A., PHD2, PixInsight, Photoshop

    Automated FlatCalibration Panel

    A motorised telescope cover with a built-in LED flat panel for the C9.25, controlled from N.I.N.A. It closes for darks and lights up for flats, so calibration frames take themselves.
