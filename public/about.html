About

I’m Adi, an astrophotographer in Bangalore. DeepSkyVision is where I share what I shoot, what I shoot it with, and what I build to shoot it better.

Imaging from the city

My telescope sits on a rooftop under Bortle 9 skies, around 18.3 magnitudes per square arcsecond. Galaxies and faint clusters are hard to pull out of that glow, so most of my effort goes into narrowband nebulae, where a filter does what a dark site would.

The setup is deliberately long-focal-length. Plate solving puts the image scale at 0.49″ per pixel, finer than Bangalore’s typical seeing of 1.5–4″, so the atmosphere sets the limit on sharpness, not the optics.

How I work

  • Narrowband first. Hα, OIII and SII through a mono camera, mapped to the SHO palette.
  • An L-Pro as luminance. A light-pollution filter stands in for a clear luminance filter.
  • Automated nights. A mini PC at the telescope runs N.I.N.A. and PHD2, with off-axis guiding.
  • PixInsight and Photoshop for calibration, stacking and finishing.

On the bench

  • Automated FlatCalibration Panel. A motorised cover and LED flat panel, to be shared as project files.
  • Infrared all-sky camera. A camera that watches the whole night and turns it into timelapses showing the Milky Way, the weather and the passing hours.
  • Rooftop pier. A permanent mount point, so the rig stays aligned from one night to the next.

What’s next

Coorg, a few hours away and far darker, is the next target for broadband galaxy work. More frames, more project files and, later, prints and processing presets will appear here as they’re ready.
